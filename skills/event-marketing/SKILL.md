# Event Marketing

Make webinars, trade shows, dinners, and talks produce measurable pipeline, authority, and reusable content, not just attendance.

> Output is a draft for human review. Nothing gets sent, posted, published, registered, purchased, or launched by this skill. Invitations, reminders, and follow-ups are drafted for a human to send.

## Before starting

Read the product marketing context document if one exists. Then establish, in one batch:
1. **Role:** hosting, sponsoring/exhibiting, speaking, or attending (mixed roles are normal; plan each separately, share one follow-up system)
2. **Primary outcome:** pipeline/meetings, authority/brand, community, or content (pick one; events that try to do everything measure nothing)
3. **Who must be in the room:** the ICP segment and roughly how many exist at this event
4. **Budget and team:** money, and who can actually work it

## Portfolio first: which events deserve investment

- **Is in-person necessary?** It earns its cost mainly for high-trust, high-ACV motions: multi-stakeholder deals, regulated or conservative buyers, heavy customization, 6+ month cycles. Otherwise lead with digital formats.
- **80/20 selection.** A handful of events usually generate most event pipeline. Double down on them (speaking slots, side events, more people, better placement) and cut the tail.
- **Bigger isn't better.** Mega-conferences dilute the audience; niche and regional events (50-200 attendees) often win on cost per qualified meeting.
- **Judge each event individually,** and judge recurring programs on a 2-3 instance trend. The first run of anything underperforms.

## The universal arc: 20% event, 80% before and after

**Before (where pipeline is made)**
- Build the target list: attendees matching the ICP (attendee and speaker lists, "who's going" posts, past attendees).
- **Book meetings before you arrive.** Specific, low-friction, time-boxed asks ("15 minutes at the coffee bar Tuesday").
- Announce your presence where your audience already is, with a reason to find you (what they get, not just a booth number).

**During**
- Optimize for qualified conversations, not raw contacts.
- **Capture context:** after each conversation, note what they said, what they care about, and the agreed next step, the same hour.
- Create content while there (see Content arc).

**After (where pipeline is won or lost)**
- **Follow up within 24-48 hours,** referencing the actual conversation; response rates decay fast.
- **Tier it:** Hot (next step agreed) → personal note + concrete next step, same/next day; Warm → personal note + one asset matched to what they said; Scan-only → one light touch or nothing. Never dump badge scans into a sales sequence.
- Whoever had the conversation writes or reviews the follow-up.
- Tag every contact with the event source in the CRM; route not-nows to nurture.

## Hosting: pick the format for the job

| Format | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Webinar | Lead gen + education at scale | Flagship repeatable format (funnel below) |
| Workshop | Activation, product-qualified leads | Hands-on beats presentation |
| Exec dinner / small gathering | ABM and executive relationships | 8-14 seats, curated mix, no pitch; often the best meetings-per-dollar format |
| Meetup series | Local community presence | Consistency beats production value |
| Virtual summit | List building via partner audiences | Every speaker promotes |
| User conference | Retention, expansion, category authority | Only once a community would attend without being begged |

Two rules that outrank format: **the topic is the targeting** (pick topics your buyer would attend even if they'd never buy), and **recurring beats one-off** (budget for the third instance before running the first).

## Webinar funnel

Directional B2B ranges (your own trend is the real baseline): visitor→registrant 30-50% warm / 10-25% cold; registrant→attendee 35-45%; hold past minute 40 50-70%; attendee→next step 5-15% for a soft CTA.

**0. Topic and offer:** a problem-aware topic ("How [ICP] does X without Y"), the transformation in the title, and the post-webinar next step decided *before* writing content. One topic, one promise, one offer.

**1. Registration:** headline = the promise; subhead = who it's for and what they'll be able to do; 3-5 outcome bullets; one tight speaker-credibility block; timezone-aware date with "register anyway for the replay"; short form (name, email, at most one qualifying field). Promote starting ~2 weeks out: 3 list emails (announcement, value preview, day-of last call), host personal posts (outperform brand posts), co-hosts' and speakers' audiences (handle consent for shared registrant lists), paid only after the topic is proven.

**2. Show-up:** calendar add at registration (the highest-leverage fix); reminders at confirmation, T-1 day (tease a specific insight), T-1 hour, and **T-5 minutes with the join link**; keep registration close to the event or add a mid-window touch; ask a question at registration (content input + micro-commitment). Mid-week, late morning or early afternoon in the audience's main timezone.

**3. Live-to-close:**
1. Open (0-5 min): restate the promise and say the offer comes at the end (naming it upfront removes the ambush feeling)
2. Content (5-35 min): teach the real thing in 3 points, each with proof; polls and pattern breaks every ~7 minutes
3. Transition (1 min, scripted word for word): the product as the implementation of what you just taught
4. Offer (5-8 min): one concrete next step and a *real* reason to act now (never fake scarcity)
5. Q&A (10+ min): questions are objections in disguise; seed 2-3 starters; restate the offer mid-Q&A and at close

**4. Post-webinar (half the value):** attended and engaged → personal follow-up within 24h referencing their question; left early → replay with a timestamp to what they missed; no-show and replay-registrants → replay sequence (2-3 emails) with a deadline. Pick a time-limited replay for offers or an evergreen replay as a lead magnet. Recycle the transcript into a recap post, clips, and quotable stats.

**Diagnose:** low registration → topic/title/promise; low show-up (<30%) → reminders or registration-to-event gap (check calendar adds first); low hold → front-loading or promise mismatch (find the drop-off minute); high hold, low conversion → transition or offer.

## Sponsoring and exhibiting

**Should we sponsor?** (before looking at prices)
1. ICP overlap in absolute numbers (ask for attendee breakdown by role and company type)
2. Meetings math backwards: total cost (sponsorship + travel + staff time + booth) ÷ realistic qualified meetings = **cost per qualified meeting**; compare against outbound and paid
3. Strategic multipliers that justify a premium: buyers concentrated nowhere else, conspicuous absence, access you can't buy elsewhere
4. Counterfactual: what would the same budget do in your best channel?
Red flags: attendee counts without composition, impressions as the headline metric, leads defined as scans, prior sponsors who didn't return.

**Negotiate, in order of value:** a speaking or panel slot; side-event rights; what attendee data is actually deliverable (in writing); placement near traffic; price last (late inventory discounts).

**The side-event play:** host a curated dinner or breakfast for 8-14 hand-picked ICP attendees adjacent to the conference, invited personally 2-4 weeks out, no pitch. Often deeper meetings at a fraction of a booth's cost, and it doesn't require sponsoring.

**Working a booth:** staff with people who can qualify and demo; shift schedules; a 30-second qualifying question ("What does your team use for X today?"); one clear message on the booth (the problem, not a feature list); a book-a-meeting QR to a calendar; booth as the venue for pre-booked meetings.

## Speaking

- **Get booked:** pitch the audience takeaway, not your company. Title = specific outcome + specific audience + tension or number. Abstract under ~150 words: the problem as they feel it, 2-3 concrete takeaways, proof you've done it. Ladder from meetups to niche tracks to main stages; recordings of small talks are your portfolio. Late panel and replacement slots are often open.
- **Design the talk:** (1) outline: who it's for, the "Monday takeaway" (what they can do on a specific next day), what you get from it, and blocks that each have a point and a proof; (2) storyboard the feeling: 5-8 beats for a 20-minute talk, each with energy, the audience's feeling in their words, and the one thing it must land. Check that the sequence of feelings earns the takeaway.
- **The recording is the real audience:** speak key numbers, your company next to your category, and your quotable line out loud at an energy peak (transcripts get crawled and cited by AI assistants). Confirm you'll receive and may republish the recording.
- **Around the talk:** invite specific people beforehand; meet other speakers; end with one simple pointer (slides + a related asset); note every Q&A question; follow up with askers within 24-48 hours.

## Attending (no booth, no stage)

Build a list of 15-30 named targets; pre-book 15-minute meetings 1-3 weeks out; host a small side dinner; attend sessions where targets speak and ask a real question; same 24-48h follow-up.

## Content arc

Record what you're allowed to; publish transcripts and recaps; clip peak moments; pull quotes for social; collect testimonials on site; feed proof points to sales enablement.

## Measurement

| Tier | Examples | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Vanity | Registrations, scans, foot traffic, impressions | Track; never report as success |
| Real | Qualified conversations, meetings, opportunities, pipeline influenced | The scoreboard |
| Decisive | Cost per qualified meeting, cost per opportunity, closed-won influenced | Decides whether you do it again |

Use source tags plus self-reported attribution ("met you at X") with an influence window matching the sales cycle; never claim last-click credit for a deal the event merely touched.

## Common mistakes

Sponsoring for "awareness" with no meetings target; the follow-up gap; fixing show-up when the topic is weak; one-off thinking; skipping the recording; counting scans as leads; writing off all events after one bad one; chasing the biggest conferences.
