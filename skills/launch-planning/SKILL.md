# Launch Planning

Plan launches that build momentum, capture attention, and convert interest into users and pipeline. Strong teams launch again and again: every feature, improvement, and update is a chance to re-engage the audience.

> Output is a draft plan and draft assets for human review. Nothing gets sent, posted, scheduled, published, or launched by this skill.

## Before starting

Read the product marketing context document if one exists. Then ask only what's missing:
1. What's launching? (new product, major feature, minor update)
2. Current audience size and engagement
3. Owned channels available (email list, blog, community, podcast)
4. Timeline
5. Past launches: what worked and what didn't
6. Any launch platforms or events under consideration

## Readiness gate (run first)

Two failure modes kill launches from opposite ends:
- **Stealth mode:** launching too late, polishing in private until nobody knows you exist.
- **"Just one more feature":** every launch date slips for one more thing.

The middle path is **Simple, Lovable, Complete (SLC)**: a launchable v1 does one clear job (simple), is something users would pick rather than endure (lovable), and is a whole experience at its chosen scope with no glaring stubs (complete).

Checklist:
- [ ] Does it do one clearly defined thing?
- [ ] Would a target user pick it, not just tolerate it?
- [ ] Is that one thing complete?
- [ ] Polishing past this bar? → stop and ship.
- [ ] Still adding scope? → cut back to SLC.

## Channel plan: Owned, Rented, Borrowed

Everything should lead back to owned channels.

- **Owned** (email list, blog, podcast, community, website/product): compounding, no algorithm risk. Start with 1-2 that fit the audience.
- **Rented** (LinkedIn, X, YouTube, communities, marketplaces): speed, not stability. Pick 1-2 where the audience is active and use them to drive people into owned channels (e.g., high-value LinkedIn posts → newsletter or gated asset).
- **Borrowed** (guest posts, podcast interviews, newsletter features, co-hosted webinars, conference talks, partner co-marketing, creators): instant credibility. Be proactive: list the voices your audience follows, pitch win-win collaborations, then convert borrowed attention into owned relationships.

## Five-phase rollout

1. **Internal:** recruit early users one-on-one; collect usability gaps; a demo-able prototype is enough.
2. **Alpha:** early-access landing page and signup; announce it exists; invite testers individually; working in production even if evolving.
3. **Beta:** work through the early-access list; teaser marketing about the problem it solves; recruit friendly customers, investors, and advocates to try and share; "beta" labels in-product.
4. **Early access:** share screenshots, GIFs, demos; gather usage data and qualitative feedback; run interviews with engaged users; optionally a product/market fit survey to refine messaging. Expand in batches or all at once under "early access" framing.
5. **Full launch:** open availability (and pricing); announce across all channels.

**Launch touchpoints:** customer email, in-app announcement or tour, website banner, "new" label in-product, blog post, social posts, sales enablement for the field (talk track, one-pager, FAQ, objection handling), relevant launch or review platforms, and partner/borrowed placements.

## Launch-platform day (e.g., a community launch site)

- **Before:** build relationships with supporters and communities; polish the listing (clear tagline, visuals, short demo video); study successful launches; contribute value in communities before pitching; staff the day.
- **On the day:** treat it as an all-day event; respond to every comment; spark discussion; point traffic to your site to capture signups.
- **After:** follow up with everyone who engaged; convert the traffic into owned relationships; keep momentum with post-launch content.

Pros: early-adopter reach, credibility, backlinks. Cons: competitive, short-lived spikes, heavy prep.

## Post-launch

- Onboarding sequence introducing key features and use cases
- Include the launch in the next roundup/newsletter for people who missed it
- Comparison pages that show why you're the obvious choice (see `competitor-comparison-pages`)
- Update product, feature, and pricing pages with the new capability
- An interactive or recorded demo so visitors can explore before talking to sales
- Feed new proof (early customer results, quotes) back into sales collateral

## Ongoing announcements: how much marketing each update deserves

| Update | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Major** (new product, major feature, overhaul) | Full campaign: blog, email, in-app, social, sales enablement, borrowed channels |
| **Medium** (integration, notable UI improvement) | Targeted: email to relevant segments, in-app banner, short post |
| **Minor** (fixes, tweaks) | Changelog and release notes |

Tactics: stagger releases to sustain momentum; reuse what resonated last time; keep signaling active development (even small changelog entries build confidence and retention). If merged changes or release notes are available, turn them into a weekly changelog draft, announcement drafts, and a pages-to-update list.

## Launch checklist

**Pre-launch**
- [ ] Landing page with a clear value proposition
- [ ] Waitlist / early-access capture and list
- [ ] Owned, rented, and borrowed channels identified
- [ ] Launch assets (screenshots, demo video, GIFs)
- [ ] Sales enablement assets ready for the field
- [ ] Onboarding flow ready
- [ ] Analytics and tracking in place
- [ ] Site QA if a new site, redesign, or migration is involved (links, forms, redirects, mobile, tracking, page speed, metadata)

**Launch day**
- [ ] Announcement email drafted and approved
- [ ] Blog post ready
- [ ] Social posts drafted for the owner to publish
- [ ] In-app announcement and website banner ready
- [ ] Team staffed to respond; monitoring for issues

**Post-launch**
- [ ] Onboarding sequence active
- [ ] Follow-up with engaged prospects
- [ ] Roundup/newsletter mention
- [ ] Comparison pages and demo published
- [ ] Feedback gathered and acted on
- [ ] Next launch moment planned

## Output

A launch plan with: readiness verdict, tier (major/medium/minor), phase timeline with owners and dates, channel plan (owned/rented/borrowed), asset list with draft copy, launch-day run sheet, post-launch follow-ups, and success metrics.
