# Sales Enablement

Create collateral reps actually use: decks, one-pagers, battlecards, objection docs, demo scripts, and playbooks that help close deals.

> Output is a draft for human review. Nothing gets sent, posted, published, or launched by this skill.

## Before starting

Read the product marketing context document if one exists. Gather what's missing:
1. **Value and differentiators:** what you sell, to whom, why you vs. the next best alternative, which outcomes you can prove
2. **Sales motion:** self-serve, inside, field, or hybrid; average deal size and cycle; personas in the decision
3. **Collateral needs:** which asset, which funnel stage, who uses it (AE, SDR, champion, prospect)
4. **Current state:** what exists, what works, what reps ask for most

## Core principles

- **Sales uses what sales trusts.** Use reps' language; test drafts with top performers. If reps rewrite it before sending, it was the wrong asset.
- **Situation-specific.** Tailor to persona, stage, and use case.
- **Scannable over comprehensive.** Reps need the answer in 3 seconds mid-call.
- **Tie to business outcomes.** Revenue, efficiency, or risk. "Cut reporting time 80%," not "AI-powered analytics."
- **Claims reps can defend.** Buyers check claims.
  - "Why we win" needs evidence (win/loss interviews, call recordings, customer quotes, or CRM reasons backed by one of those). Without it, write advantages as **hypotheses** and say what evidence would confirm them.
  - Competitor claims are dated and sourced; write "not listed on their site as of [date]," not "they don't have it"; never state their motives as fact.
  - **Include where you lose.** Reps trust a doc that admits when the competitor is the better fit.

## Battlecards

A reusable one-competitor guide for reps. **Ask which competitor** if it isn't clear; you may suggest the likely one, but don't draft until confirmed. Pull facts from `competitor-profiling` profiles when they exist.

Structure (one page):
1. **When you'll see them:** segments, deal sizes, triggers
2. **Why buyers consider them:** their real appeal, in buyers' words
3. **Where they're strong:** honest, so reps aren't blindsided
4. **Where we win, and for whom:** tied to evidence
5. **Discovery questions:** surface the fit criteria where you're stronger, without trashing them
6. **Top objections:** one-line response + proof each
7. **When to walk away:** deal profiles where they're the better fit
8. **Last verified:** date, and what changed since the last version

Keep current: re-verify pricing and packaging quarterly and whenever reps hear something new; tag every fact with source and date.

## Live deal objection

When a rep brings one objection from a live deal, answer that deal, not with a generic doc:
1. **Pin down the moment:** who said it, their role, exact words, stage, what happens next. If the rep is short on time, state assumptions and ask only what would change the answer.
2. **Name the likely real concern** and what in the deal points to it. If two readings fit, give a question that tells them apart.
3. **Give one response the rep can say as written:** acknowledge, address the real concern, one proof point, end with a question.
4. **Use only defensible proof.** Don't invent stats, customers, or competitor numbers, and don't repeat the buyer's claim about a competitor as fact ("half the price" may not be the same scope, so ask what's in their number).
5. **Say when not to fight.** If it's a real fit gap, suggest qualifying out or narrowing scope.

Suggest the rep log the objection and outcome for the library and win-loss work.

## Objection handling docs

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Price | "Too expensive," "No budget this quarter," "Competitor is cheaper" |
| Timing | "Not now," "Next quarter," "Too busy to implement" |
| Competition | "We already use X," "What makes you different?" |
| Authority | "I need to check with my boss," "The committee decides" |
| Status quo | "What we have works fine" |
| Technical | "Does it integrate with X?", security, scale |

For each: the objection as reps hear it → why they say it (real concern) → response approach → proof point → follow-up question.
Two formats: a **quick-reference table** for live calls (fits one screen) and a **detailed doc** for prep and training (talk tracks, role-plays).

## Win-loss analysis

1. **Inventory sources and their bias:** CRM loss reasons (rep-entered, skewed to price/timing), call transcripts, buyer interviews (most candid, smallest sample).
2. **Include wins** against the same competitors.
3. **Segment before counting:** by competitor, segment, deal size, stage lost.
4. **Code each deal** with one primary reason (+ secondaries), a supporting quote, and the source. Keep coding consistent (one coder, or double-code a sample).
5. **Report counts with n:** "6 of 9 losses to X cite onboarding time." Under ~5 deals is a signal to watch, not a finding.
6. **Turn findings into assets:** confirm one-word CRM reasons with transcripts or interviews before changing a battlecard; then update battlecards, objection library, and discovery questions; route product gaps to product.

Report: scope and sample, findings with counts and quotes, by-competitor breakdown, recommended asset updates.

## Sales / pitch deck (10-12 slides)

1. Current-world problem → 2. Cost of the problem → 3. The shift creating urgency → 4. Your approach → 5. Product walkthrough (3-4 workflows, not a feature tour) → 6. Proof points → 7. One case study told well → 8. Implementation / timeline → 9. ROI / value → 10. Pricing overview → 11. Next steps with timeline

Principles: story arc, not feature tour; one idea per slide; designed for presenting, not reading.

| Buyer | Emphasize | De-emphasize |
|---|---|---|
| Technical | Architecture, security, integrations, API | ROI math |
| Economic | ROI, payback, total cost, risk | Technical detail |
| Champion | Internal selling points, quick wins, peer proof | Deep technical/financial detail |

## One-pagers / leave-behinds

Uses: post-meeting recap, champion internal selling, event handout.
Structure: problem in one sentence → your solution → 3 differentiators → one strong proof point → CTA with a specific named contact. One page (front, or front and back max), scannable in 30 seconds.

## ROI framing

- **Inputs** the prospect provides: time on manual work, current tool costs, error rates, team size
- **Calculations:** time saved, cost reduction, revenue impact
- **Outputs:** annual ROI %, payback months, 3-year value

| Persona | Cares about | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Technical leader | Architecture, scale, security, velocity | Technical fit, integration depth |
| Sales/revenue leader | Pipeline, quota, rep productivity | Revenue impact, time saved per rep |
| Finance | Total cost, payback, risk | ROI, predictability |
| End user | Ease, daily workflow, learning curve | Time saved, frustration removed |
| Operations leader | Consistency, cost control, throughput | Process standardization, waste reduction |

Formats: spreadsheet (fast, per-deal), web tool (polished, high volume), or slides (exec presentations).

## Demo scripts and talk tracks

Script: opening (2 min: agenda, confirm goals) → discovery recap (3 min) → walkthrough (15-20 min, 3-4 workflows mapped to their pain) → interaction points throughout → close (5 min: value summary, next steps with timeline).

| Call type | Length | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | 30 min | Qualify, understand pain, map buying process |
| First demo | 30-45 min | 3-4 workflows tied to their pain |
| Technical deep-dive | 45-60 min | Architecture, security, integrations |
| Executive overview | 20-30 min | Outcomes, ROI, strategic fit |

Demo after discovery, never before; use their terminology and workflow; leave time for questions.

**Cold call opener:** who you are and why you're calling, then ask permission to continue. **Voicemail:** 20-30 seconds, one specific reason, "I'll send you a note too."

## Case-study briefs (sales format)

Customer profile (industry, size, buyer role) → challenge (2-3 sentences) → solution (1-2 sentences) → 3 before/after metrics → one pull quote → tags (industry, use case, size, persona). Organize for instant retrieval by industry, use case, and size.

## Proposals

Executive summary (1 page: their challenge, your solution, expected outcome) → proposed solution mapped to requirements → implementation plan → investment (transparent pricing) → next steps and decision timeline. Aim for 5-7 pages; mirror discovery language; name stakeholders; include only relevant case studies.

## Playbooks

Buyer profile; qualification framework (e.g., BANT or MEDDIC); discovery questions by topic; top 10 objections; competitive positioning per competitor; demo flow per persona; follow-up/proposal/breakup email templates. Build for launches, new segments, and new-hire ramp. Assign an owner and review quarterly.

## Buyer persona cards

Fields: role/titles and reporting line; goals; pains; top 3-5 objections; evaluation criteria; buying-process role; the one-sentence messaging angle. Cover economic buyer, technical buyer, end user, champion, and blocker.

## Output formats

| Asset | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| Deck | Slide-by-slide outline: headline, body copy, speaker notes |
| One-pager | Full copy with layout guidance |
| Battlecard | One-page card per the structure above |
| Objection doc | Table: objection, response, proof, follow-up |
| Live objection | Likely concern, sayable response, proof, follow-up question |
| Win-loss report | Scope, findings with counts and quotes, by-competitor breakdown, asset updates |
| Demo script | Scene-by-scene with timing, talk track, interaction points |
| ROI model | Inputs, formulas, outputs with sample data |
| Playbook / persona card / proposal | Structured document per the sections above |

If context is missing, ask: which asset, who uses it, which stage, which persona, and the top 3 objections heard.
