# Conversion Copywriting

Write marketing copy that is clear, specific, supportable, and drives one action, without the patterns that make copy read as AI-written.

> Output is a draft for human review. Nothing gets sent, posted, published, or launched by this skill.

## Before writing

Read the product marketing context document if one exists, including any record of angles and claims already tested (don't re-propose those as new). Gather what's missing:
1. **Page purpose:** page type and the ONE primary action
2. **Audience:** ideal customer, the problem, objections, the words they use for the problem
3. **Offer:** what's sold, how it differs from alternatives, the key outcome, proof (numbers, testimonials, case studies)
4. **Context:** traffic source and what visitors already know

## Diagnose the situation first

| Situation | You were given | Your job |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Blank or generic** | A one-line ask or company-speak with no point of view | Build the argument from the brief and context. Latitude on angle, never on facts: no invented stats, customers, or results |
| **B. Real voice present** | A draft with an angle or recognizable voice, even if rough | Restore it: keep their argument and vocabulary; fix the buried lead, vague lines, AI tells, structure |
| **C. Point edit** | "Fix this headline," "tighten this paragraph" | Change only what was asked plus the minimum around it; return the rest untouched |

State the diagnosis in one line with evidence before writing.

**Read taste from the input (B and C):** their vocabulary is a voice choice (don't upgrade "stuff" to "solutions"); where they spent words is a priority choice; what they left out is a scope choice; their angle is the positioning, so deepen it rather than replace it. If they provide a writing sample, match its sentence length, punctuation, and paragraph openings.

## Drafting protocol

- **Decompose and lock.** Break the page into units (headline, subhead, CTA, each section heading/body, proof lines). Draft 2-3 candidates per unit, keep the best, fix its worst flaw, lock it. Then read the whole page once for flow. In Situation B, lines that already work start locked.
- **Three-question gate** for every line:
  1. Can the reader picture it?
  2. Can it be proven true or false?
  3. Could a competitor write this exact sentence? (swap test)
  Three passes: keep. Two: sharpen the weak one. One or none: rewrite from the facts or flag `[NEED: ...]`.
- **Ration the punch.** Most lines plain, specific, declarative; one or two punchy moments per page.
- **Leave-it-alone test.** For each change: measurably better, or just different? Revert changes you can't justify.

## Principles

- **Clarity over cleverness.** If the reader has to decode it, you've lost them.
- **Benefits over features:** what it means for the customer, not just what it does.
- **Specific over vague:** "Cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes," not "save time."
- **Customer language over company language:** mirror reviews, interviews, support tickets.
- **One idea per section,** building a logical flow down the page.
- **Hero as a transformation:** current discomfort → better vision → path to action. Test each headline with "Now you can [headline]" and see if it reads as a real new capability.
- **Claims must be supportable.** When a requested claim isn't supported, don't write it and don't silently drop it: name it, say why it falls short, offer the closest supportable alternative, and state the evidence that would unlock it (a review export, a written guarantee policy, a certification, a citable study).

**Style rules:** simple over complex ("use," not "make use of"); specific over vague (avoid streamline, optimize, innovative); active voice; confident (drop almost/very/really); show over tell; honest over sensational; no exclamation points.

## No AI tells

**Never write:**
1. **Contrast reveals:** "It's not X, it's Y," "Not just X, but Y." State Y directly, with the reason.
2. **Negation lists:** "No setup, no templates, no waiting." Say what does happen; state one real absence near the CTA if needed ("No card required").
3. **Trailing pile-ons:** a full claim followed by a comma and more restating clauses. End at the claim.
4. **Self-answered questions and colon reveals:** "The result? 3x faster." "The best part: it learns." Just say it. (FAQ questions and a reader's own question are fine.)
5. **Stock phrases:** "In today's fast-paced world," "Whether you're X or Y," "Here's the thing," "Say goodbye to," "X, reimagined," "Unlock the power of," "Take it to the next level."
6. **Em dashes in short copy** (headlines, subheads, ads, social posts, subject lines).

**Keep rare:** at most one fragment and one list of three per section; words like seamless, robust, powerful, unlock (replace with the fact they stand for).

**Build a writer** before a full Situation A rewrite: who is writing and why; what they know cold (most words and detail); what they'd compress or cut; their honest, arguable opinion; what they'd cut first if forced to lose a third. This produces uneven density, which is human. Equal coverage of everything is a tell.

**Self-check before delivering:**
1. Search headlines and short copy for "not," "isn't," "no," "without," "?", ":" and "—"; check each hit against the bans.
2. Reread every sentence over 20 words; more than two commas suggests a pile-on.
3. Count fragments, threes, and filler vocabulary per section.
4. Read it aloud; mark anything that sounds like a press release.
5. Apply the swap test to headline, subhead, and section headings.
6. Check for second-generation tells: tidy closers at the end of each section, wisdom-shaped sentences, suspiciously equal section lengths.

Fix from facts, not synonyms. Flag missing proof as `[NEED: ...]` rather than inventing it.

## Page structure

**Above the fold**
- **Headline:** the single most important message. Formulas to start from (fill every slot with specifics):
  - "{Outcome} without {pain}" / "{Outcome} by {mechanism}" / "Turn {input} into {outcome}" / "{Outcome} in {timeframe}"
  - "Never {unpleasant event} again" / "{Question naming the pain}" / "Stop [pain]. Start [pleasure]."
  - "{Product type} for {audience}" / "You don't have to {skill} to {outcome}"
  - "The {category} that {differentiator}" / "[Number] [people] use [product] to [outcome]"
- **Subheadline:** 1-2 sentences adding specificity
- **Primary CTA:** what they get ("Start Free Trial" > "Sign Up")

**Core sections:** social proof (logos, stats, testimonials) · problem/pain · solution/benefits (3-5) · how it works (3-4 steps) · objection handling (FAQ, comparisons, guarantees) · final CTA (recap, repeat CTA, risk reversal).

**CTAs:** avoid Submit, Sign Up, Learn More, Click Here, Get Started. Formula: [action verb] + [what they get] + [qualifier] ("Get the Complete Checklist," "See Pricing for My Team").

## Page-specific guidance

- **Homepage:** serve several audiences without going generic; lead with the broadest value proposition; clear paths per intent.
- **Landing page:** one message, one CTA; match the headline to the traffic source; complete argument on one page.
- **Pricing page:** help visitors pick a plan; address "which is right for me?"; make the recommended plan obvious.
- **Feature page:** feature → benefit → outcome; use cases; clear path to try or buy.
- **About page:** why you exist, mission tied to customer benefit, still a CTA.

**Voice and tone:** set formality (casual / professional-friendly / formal) and personality (playful vs. serious, bold vs. understated, technical vs. accessible). Headlines can be bolder; body clearer; CTAs action-oriented.

## Output

- **Point edit (C):** only the changed lines, with a one-line reason each.
- **Page copy:** organized by section (headline, subhead, CTA, section headers and body, secondary CTAs); short annotations on key choices; 2-3 headline and CTA alternatives with rationale; page title and meta description if relevant; open `[NEED: ...]` flags listed at the end.
