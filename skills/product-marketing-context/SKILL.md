# Product Marketing Context

Create and maintain one shared **product marketing context document** that captures positioning and messaging, so every other marketing skill (copy, outbound, enablement, launches, AI search) starts from the same facts instead of re-asking.

> Output is a draft for human review. Nothing gets sent, posted, published, or launched by this skill. Changes to the shared document are saved only after the user approves them.

## Step 1: Check for an existing document

**If it exists:** summarize what's captured, its current version, and the last few changelog entries; ask which sections to update; gather only for those.

**If it doesn't:** offer two paths:
1. **Auto-draft (recommended):** study existing materials (website and landing pages, decks, one-pagers, about page, meta descriptions, recent case studies, sales call notes the user provides) and draft V1 for the user to correct.
2. **From scratch:** walk through the sections conversationally, one at a time (explain what you're capturing, ask, confirm, move on). Don't dump every question at once.

Push for **verbatim customer language**; exact phrases beat polished descriptions.

## Sections to capture

1. **Product overview:** one-liner; what it does (2-3 sentences); category (the "shelf" customers search on); product type; business model and pricing
2. **Target audience:** company type (industry, size, stage); decision-makers; primary use case; 2-3 jobs to be done; specific scenarios
3. **Personas (B2B):** user, champion, decision maker, financial buyer, technical influencer: what each cares about, their challenge, the value promised
4. **Problems and pain:** core challenge before finding you; why current solutions fall short; what it costs (time, money, opportunity); emotional tension
5. **Competitive landscape:** direct (same solution, same problem), secondary (different solution, same problem), indirect (conflicting approach, e.g., spreadsheets or doing nothing), and how each falls short
6. **Differentiation:** key differentiators, how you solve it differently, why that's better, why customers pick you
7. **Objections and anti-personas:** top 3 sales objections with responses; who is NOT a fit
8. **Switching dynamics (JTBD four forces):** push (frustrations with the current way), pull (what attracts them to you), habit (what keeps them stuck), anxiety (what worries them about switching)
9. **Customer language:** how they describe the problem and your solution (verbatim); words to use and avoid; glossary
10. **Brand voice:** 3-5 attributes (each with what it means, what it doesn't, and a real example line); tone by context (support, launch, errors, sales, social); terms you own; words you never use and why. Build from copy that performed and leaders' own writing, not adjectives picked in a meeting.
11. **Proof points:** metrics, notable customers, testimonial snippets, value themes with supporting evidence
12. **Goals:** primary business goal, key conversion action, current metrics
13. **Paid ads (optional):** target CPA/ROAS and hard floor, budget and pacing, which conversions count and their value, tracking weaknesses, campaign purposes, and what an agent may do (read, compare, flag, draft, recommend) vs. may not do without approval (change budgets or bids, pause or launch, edit tracking, publish)
14. **Learnings:** starts empty; fills as work ships

## Step 2: Confirm, version, and save

- Show the completed document and ask for adjustments.
- **New document:** version v1 with a single changelog entry.
- **Update:** increment the version, update "Last updated," and prepend one changelog line naming the sections touched and why (e.g., "v3 - Repositioned from X to Y; added a new persona after 5 customer interviews"). Never rewrite past entries. Typo-only fixes don't bump the version.
- If the change is a real repositioning, say so plainly, since downstream skills will now generate against it.

## Maintaining learnings

The changelog records how the document changed; **Learnings** records what the team found out by shipping, so drafting skills start from what's known.

- **Format:** one specific sentence that would change a draft; evidence with numbers and sample size; what it applies to; status `active`, `watch` (one data point), or `retired`.
- **Earns a place:** a test that reached its planned sample, a pattern seen in 2+ campaigns, a decision with a reason that drafts must respect, or a correction given more than once.
- **Doesn't:** single anecdotes, task notes, or items that belong in another section (new objections go to Objections; new phrases to Customer Language).
- **Retire, don't delete,** when contradicted, made obsolete by a product or market change, or irrelevant for two quarters, with a line on why. Keep ~20 active rows.
- Any skill may *propose* a learning; add it only on user approval, with a version bump.

## Tips

Ask specific questions ("What's the #1 frustration that brings them to you?"); ask for examples; summarize and confirm each section; skip sections that don't apply.
