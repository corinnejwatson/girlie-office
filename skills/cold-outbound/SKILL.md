# Cold Outbound

Write outbound that sounds like a sharp, thoughtful human (not a sales machine following a template), works alongside other channels, and turns replies into meetings. Upstream list building and account research live in `prospecting-list-building` and `account-research-signal-plays`.

> Output is a draft for human review. Nothing gets sent, posted, connected, enrolled, or launched by this skill. A human reviews, loads, and sends every touch.

## Before writing

If a product marketing context document exists, read it first. Then understand (ask only if missing):
1. **Who:** role, company, why them specifically
2. **The ask:** meeting, reply, intro, demo
3. **The value:** the specific problem you solve for people like them
4. **Proof:** a result, case study, or credibility signal
5. **Signals:** funding, hiring, posts, news, tech stack changes, account brief

Don't block on missing inputs. Write with what you have and note what would make it stronger.

## Writing principles

- **Peer, not vendor.** Contractions; read it aloud; if it sounds like marketing copy, rewrite it.
- **Every sentence earns its place.** The best cold emails feel like they could have been shorter.
- **Tailoring must connect to the problem.** If you delete the personal opener and the email still makes sense, the tailoring isn't working.
- **Their world, not yours.** "You/your" dominates "I/we." Don't open with who you are.
- **One low-friction ask.** Interest CTAs ("Worth exploring?") beat meeting requests. One CTA, answerable in one line.

**Calibrate to the audience:** C-suite = ultra-brief, peer-level, understated; mid-level = more specific value; technical = precise, no fluff.

## Tailoring levels

1. **Basic** (name, company, title): table stakes, ~no lift
2. **Industry/segment:** segment-specific pains, trends, regulation
3. **Role:** challenges specific to their role and seniority
4. **Individual (gold standard):** a specific, timely observation about them, connected to the problem you solve ("Noticed you're hiring 3 SDRs. Teams scaling that fast usually hit follow-up fatigue during onboarding.")

**Fast system:** pick your top ~10 triggers (5 company, 5 person), write a reusable tailoring paragraph for each that segues into the problem, and keep the rest of the email constant.

**Rules for AI-assisted tailoring:** relevance beats cleverness; facts first, angle second (with a source for every fact); "no hook found" is allowed (fall back to the segment message); apply the "So what?" test to every opener. Avoid fake-feeling moves: praising the company mission, congratulating on a year-old raise, "I came across your profile," attention hacks unrelated to the problem, or creepy over-tailoring.

## Structures that work

- **Observation → Problem → Proof → Ask**
- **Question → Value → Ask**
- **Trigger → Insight → Ask**
- **Story → Bridge → Ask**
- **Problem → Agitate → Solution (PAS):** the default workhorse for problem-aware prospects
- **Before → After → Bridge:** clear transformation offers
- **Situation → Complication → Question:** consultative; mirrors how leaders present
- **Observation + binary value question:** 1-2 sentences total, for maximum brevity

Or write freeform if it flows without a framework.

## Subject lines

2-4 words, lowercase, internal-looking ("hiring ops," "q2 forecast"). No pitch, urgency, emojis, or the prospect's first name. No fake "Re:"/"Fwd:".

## Follow-up sequence

- **3-5 emails total**, widening gaps: Day 0, 3, 7-8, 14, 21-28
- **Rotate angles**, one new value point per email: (1) hook + core value + soft CTA; (2) a different angle or new insight; (3) social proof from a similar company; (4) industry trend or useful resource; (5) breakup
- Each email stands alone; never "just checking in," "bumping this," or "did you see my last email?"
- **Breakup email:** acknowledge the silence, validate that it may not be a priority, say it's the last note for now, leave the door open (optionally the "reply 1/2/3" format). If you send a breakup, honor it.
- **CTA by seniority:** executives get ultra-low effort ("Curious?"); managers get more specific value.
- Suggested timing: Tue-Thu, 9-11am or 1-3pm in the prospect's time zone.

## LinkedIn steps (drafts for manual execution)

- **Profile first:** headline says who you help and how; About opens with the problem; one useful Featured item; recent activity.
- **Sequence:** Day 1 view and genuinely engage with a recent post → Day 2-3 connection request → after acceptance, a short no-pitch message tied to why you connected → 3-5 days later, a value message → 5-7 days later, a light ask. Stop at three unanswered messages.
- **Connection note:** under ~200 characters, only the real reason you're connecting; skip the note if there's no credible reason.
- **First message:** ~150-200 characters, reference the reason, ask something easy.
- **Limits and risk:** keep connection requests around 15-25/day (~100/week) and acceptance above ~25-30%. Third-party LinkedIn automation violates LinkedIn's User Agreement and risks the account; prefer manual or human-assisted execution, especially for Tier 1 and for personal profiles that are business assets.

## Multichannel cadence (by tier)

| Tier | Channels |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Email, LinkedIn, phone, personal video; direct mail after engagement |
| Tier 2 | Email + LinkedIn (~14 days); phone for best-fit accounts |
| Tier 3 | Email sequence only |

Example 21-day Tier 1 plan: D1 engage on LinkedIn + email 1 (short, no link); D3 connection request; D4 email 2 in-thread; D6 call 1 + voicemail + recap email; D8 LinkedIn message or short personal video; D11 email 3 (new angle or case study); D13 call 2; D15 comment on a new post; D18 email 4 (breakup or referral ask); D21 call 3, then nurture.

**Cadence rules:** a reply on any channel stops every channel; an opt-out on one channel is an opt-out on all; one sender per account at a time; each touch makes sense alone; max 4-7 emails and 3 LinkedIn messages; never two channels on the same day except a voicemail and its recap.

**Calls:** say who you are and why you're calling, then ask permission to continue; voicemail 20-30 seconds with one specific reason and "I'll send you a note too." **Video:** 60-120 seconds, show their site/product on screen early; Tier 1 or post-reply only. **SMS/WhatsApp:** never for cold outreach; only after opt-in.

## Suggested replies (drafts)

| Reply type | Suggested handling |
|---|---|
| Positive | Draft a fast response proposing two specific times in their time zone plus a booking-link fallback |
| Information request | Answer the actual question briefly, then offer a call |
| Objection | Respond once, briefly (see below) |
| Not now | Thank them, note the follow-up date they gave, stop the sequence |
| Referral | Thank them; draft a note to the referred person citing the referral |
| Wrong person | Ask once who owns it; otherwise stop |
| Out of office | Pause until their return date |
| Unsubscribe / hostile | Flag for suppression on every channel; never argue |
| Bounce | Flag for removal and a possible job-change check |

**Early objections:** "We use [competitor]" → acknowledge, name the one situation where teams like theirs still look elsewhere, ask if it applies. "No budget" → ask when they plan budgets, or offer something budget-free (audit, benchmark). "Not a priority" → ask what is; step back if unconnected. "Send info" → one specific asset plus one easy question. "How did you get my email?" → answer honestly with the source and offer removal.

## Sending-readiness checklist (for whoever sends)

Include this note with sequences so the human sender can confirm: secondary sending domains with SPF/DKIM/DMARC and warmed mailboxes; list verified right before upload and bounces under 2%; open/click tracking off (measure replies); plain-text opt-out line and postal address in every email; suppression list checked.

## What to avoid

- "I hope this finds you well," "My name is X and I work at Y"
- Jargon: synergy, leverage, circle back, best-in-class, leading provider
- Feature dumps (one proof point beats ten features)
- **AI tells:** contrast reveals ("It's not X, it's Y"), "no X, no Y, no Z" lists, self-answered questions ("The result? 40% more meetings."), colon reveals, stock phrases ("Here's the thing," "Unlock," "Take it to the next level"), em dashes
- HTML, images, multiple links; templates with only the first name swapped; 30-minute call asks on first touch

## Quality check

Does it sound human read aloud? Would you reply? Does every sentence serve the reader? Is the tailoring tied to the problem? Is there exactly one easy ask?
